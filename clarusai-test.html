Context

Typing a prompt feels free. It isn’t.

Using AI feels as simple as typing and pressing enter. As the technology grows, so does the energy it takes to run it, and none of that shows up on screen.

Challenge

Concept

Every answer has to be earned.

Visitors type a question, then power the answer with their own hands. Three mechanisms turn their movement into energy, the acrylic tower lights up as it fills, and the answer arrives with what it cost.

Process

From paper to frosted acrylic in four passes.

Sketching

We narrowed broad ideas about sustainability down to one: visitors generate the energy themselves, with three mechanical movements like churning and pushing.

Winter Show

Shown at the IMA/ITP Winter Show.

The response was overwhelmingly positive. People liked earning their energy in a fun way, which made a serious topic easier to approach, and many left suggestions for where to take it next.

What I’d do next

Put the energy in units people already feel.

This was one of the biggest pieces of feedback from the show. People kept asking what 0.24 Wh actually means, because a number on its own doesn’t say much. They wanted to see it compared to something real, so next I’d set it next to things people already know.

86seconds

Rough equivalents from the 0.24 Wh median: a 10 W LED bulb, a 13 Wh phone battery, a 1,000 W microwave.

Reflection

Fail in cardboard first.

Lo-fi prototypes caught every sizing mistake before it was cut in acrylic. On a project this big, testing cheap is what made the real build work.

A good partner raises the work.

Thank you to Jiyoon, for showing me how much better a project gets when two people push it together.
