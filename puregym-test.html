Decisions

Three calls that kept the check-in screen calm.

Instead of stacking the guest pass under mine, I made it a toggle. Testers picked it

Most testers preferred Version B: Just me and Guest pass side by side. Bringing a friend is one tap, and your own code never gets pushed off the screen.

Instead of one fixed gym, members keep a list. Version 3

Some members train at more than one PureGym. Saved gyms sit behind the gym name, and adding one is a search and a pin, not a hunt through every club in the city.

Instead of piling stats onto check-in, I gave them their own tab. From testing

One tester loved the achievements, another didn’t care. So hours, streaks and achievements live in Progress, and check-in stays the code and how busy the gym is.

Reflection

01

Talk to the people at the door.

I was nervous to walk up to strangers at the gym. Most were kind, and two of them turned out to be UI/UX designers who were especially helpful.

02

Borrow the patterns people already know.

Bottom sheets, segmented toggles and wallet passes are familiar for a reason. Grounding the design in them let testers focus on the gym, not the app.
